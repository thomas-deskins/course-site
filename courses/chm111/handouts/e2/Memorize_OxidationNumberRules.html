Rules for Assigning an Oxidation Number (O.N.) General Rules 1. For an atom in its elemental form (Na, C, O2, Clo, Py, etc.): O.N. = 0. 2. For a monatomic ion: O.N. = ion charge (with the sign before the numeral; e.g. Mg** has an ON. of +2). 3. The sum of O.N. values for the atoms in a molecule or formula unit of a compound equals zero. The sum of O.N. values for the atoms in a polyatomic ion equals the ion’s charge. (e.g. Carbonate CO3- ONcarbon +3 ONoxygen= —2) For Group 1: O.N. = +1 in all compounds. For Group 2: O.N. = +2 in all compounds. For Hydrogen: O.N. = +1 in combination with nonmetals. For Fluorine: For Oxygen: For Group 17 (other halogens): O.N. =-1 in all compounds. O.N. =~-1 in peroxides (e.g., H2O>); O.N. = -2 in all other compounds (except with F). O.N. =-1 in combination with metals, nonmetals (except O), and halogens lower in the group. Prof. TK. Deskins
